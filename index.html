Why AI literacy, and why now?

AI tools are already part of how people study, work, and search for information. Waiting for a perfect curriculum means falling behind. The best way to start is to start: try the tools, ask questions, check the answers, and build something small.

Start now, not someday

You don't need to be technical. A first real conversation with an AI tool teaches more than reading about one.

Learn alongside your kids

Parents don't have to be experts to guide young people. Exploring together builds trust and good habits for everyone.

Thinking stays human

AI is a partner, not a replacement for imagination, creativity, or critical thinking. We practise checking sources and questioning answers.

Local and practical

Workshops are run in person in the Okanagan, so you can ask questions, try things, and learn with your community.

Who it's for

Families and parents

Learn how AI tools work, what they get wrong, and how to set sensible, age-appropriate guidelines at home. Try tools together with your kids.

Teens and students

Hands-on labs to compare tools, practise writing good prompts, understand academic integrity, and build a small project with AI as a thinking partner.

Small businesses

Practical uses for everyday work such as writing, research, and customer communication, plus how to choose the right tool for each job.

Teachers and schools

Professional development and school programs are being planned. Get in touch if you would like to be part of the conversation.

What you'll learn

1

Get started with confidence

What AI is and isn't, how chatbots work in plain language, and how to have a useful first conversation.

2

Compare ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini

Try the same task in different tools, side by side, and see where each one is strong, weak, or simply different.

3

Check, question, verify

Spot mistakes and made-up answers, check sources, and keep your own reasoning at the centre.

4

Privacy and safe use

What not to type into a chatbot, where your data may go, and why age rules and school policies matter.

5

Build something real

Finish with a small project you made yourself, such as a study aid, a plan, or a helper for a task you do every week.

Our approach

Hands-on, in person

Learners use real tools during the session. Less slide-watching, more trying, questioning, and building.

More than one tool

No single tool is best at everything. Learning to compare them is a skill that lasts as the tools change.

Age-appropriate and privacy-aware

Each AI tool sets its own age limits, and we respect them. We keep personal and student information out of consumer chatbots.

Honest and independent

We aren't affiliated with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, and we don't sell subscriptions to any of them.
